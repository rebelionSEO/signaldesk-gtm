# Channels, growth systems and commercial measurement

Verified: 2026-09-28. All 15 primary-source pages below were opened and inspected. Access date for every entry: **2026-09-28**. These are short method notes, not copied articles. Public availability does not establish independent evidence or effectiveness. Client facts require separate client evidence.

Use a few relevant entries alongside the [operating standard](../../process/GTM_OPERATING_STANDARD.md) and [core library](../GTM_LIBRARY.md). An ordinary tactic below is an ingredient, not a finished creative experiment. Do not spend, launch outreach, subscribe, or change prices because a reference recommends it.

## C01 — How we got our first 1,000 users

- **Source:** [James Hawkins, PostHog](https://posthog.com/founders/first-1000-users).
- **Publication:** 2024-06-21. **Access:** public founder article.
- **Topics:** developer GTM, PLG, onboarding, community, pricing.
- **Mechanism:** manually onboard early users, learn whether strangers return, then reduce signup/deployment friction. The account connects product decisions with distribution and pricing rather than treating marketing as a separate layer.
- **SignalDesk use:** propose a small observed-use test before scaling acquisition. Capture why people return, what they cannot complete, and whether the intended value is actually experienced.
- **Limits:** retrospective founder account with survivorship bias, not a controlled study. Venture-backed open-source circumstances do not transfer automatically to an agency. Do not recommend delaying revenue or copy its reported growth as a forecast.

## C02 — Six cold email examples

- **Source:** [Jonathan Costet, Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-email-examples).
- **Publication:** 2022-04-22; last modified 2026-03-04. **Access:** public article and examples.
- **Topics:** outbound, creative participation, sales conversations.
- **Mechanism:** examples combine prospect relevance with unexpected physical or visual elements, rather than relying only on a feature pitch.
- **SignalDesk use:** separate the relevant reason to engage from the attention device. Test whether the recipient finds the object or message useful before scaling a creative outreach concept.
- **Limits:** selected success examples, no representative comparison group. Replies and booked meetings are not revenue. Gifts, puns, caricatures and persistence are not inherently appropriate; this is not permission to send unsolicited campaigns or ignore opt-outs. An update date does not make old examples new 2026 evidence.

## C03 — How to define success for your partner marketing program

- **Source:** [Luke Swanek, PartnerStack](https://blog.partnerstack.com/post/how-to-define-success-for-your-partner-marketing-program).
- **Publication:** 2018-11-29. **Access:** public original article.
- **Topics:** partnerships, co-marketing, enablement, measurement.
- **Mechanism:** partner selection, mutual incentives and ongoing support jointly determine program design; a universal success benchmark is inappropriate.
- **SignalDesk use:** identify a complementary partner with an evidenced shared buyer problem. Scope one jointly useful interaction, each party's contribution, qualified introductions and delivery responsibilities before proposing a large program.
- **Limits:** vendor advice promotes partner software. Old foundational guidance, not current channel performance evidence. More registered partners does not establish active distribution or incremental sales.

## C04 — How to communicate a price increase—and keep your best subscribers

- **Source:** [Daphne Tideman, RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/how-to-raise-app-prices-effectively).
- **Publication:** 2025-05-06; updated 2025-06-19. **Access:** public article.
- **Topics:** pricing, retention, packaging, communication.
- **Mechanism:** evaluate the timing, customer value and treatment of existing versus new subscribers before writing the announcement.
- **SignalDesk use:** test comprehension of an offer and its value before recommending a price change. Specify whose price changes, when, and what delivered value supports the explanation.
- **Limits:** consumer subscription examples and practitioner opinion, not evidence of B2B willingness to pay. Higher price can reduce volume or retention. Do not promise improved margin without delivery cost data or treat narrative examples as causal proof.

## C05 — How we decide which moments get a message

- **Source:** [Molly Evola, Customer.io](https://customer.io/learn/lifecycle-marketing/event-based-messaging).
- **Publication:** not displayed in inspected text. **Access:** public operator article.
- **Topics:** email, in-app messaging, lifecycle, suppression.
- **Mechanism:** the same action can warrant different treatment depending on customer history; repeat content downloads need not restart the same nurture sequence.
- **SignalDesk use:** define an event, relevant history, useful response, suppression rule and exit condition. Connect an organic or paid visit to opted-in follow-up without repeatedly treating an existing customer as a new lead.
- **Limits:** describes the vendor's own workflow. A content download or engagement score is not verified purchase intent. Requires reliable first-party events and appropriate contact permissions; without these, report the dependency as missing.

## C06 — 2026 customer messaging trends: the complete data

- **Source:** [Customer.io research and methodology](https://customer.io/learn/lifecycle-marketing/2026-customer-messaging-trends).
- **Publication:** report labeled 2026; exact release date not displayed in inspected text. Supplementary platform data covers November–December 2025. **Access:** public report.
- **Topics:** email, cross-channel messaging, research quality.
- **Mechanism:** compare messaging choices by business model, role and lifecycle purpose rather than prescribing one channel for every company.
- **SignalDesk use:** use the questionnaire to identify unanswered operational questions: which messages support activation, retention or monetization, and what outcome is tracked?
- **Limits:** vendor-run, self-reported survey with a North American/mid-market B2B SaaS skew; not a client baseline or causal effectiveness study. Some demographic figures differ between marketer-specific summary and all-respondent tables. Preserve subgroup denominators rather than mixing them. No survey percentage becomes a company forecast.

## C07 — Growth loops are the new funnels

- **Source:** [Reforge; Brian Balfour, Casey Winters, Kevin Kwok and Andrew Chen](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops).
- **Publication:** article displays 2018-07-31. The collection listing displays 2026-01-09; use the article date, not the listing as evidence of new research. **Access:** public article; linked courses not accessed.
- **Topics:** connected channels, referrals, product, distribution.
- **Mechanism:** ask how the output of one cohort can create useful input for the next, spanning product, acquisition and monetization.
- **SignalDesk use:** draw a proposed route from customer value to a shareable useful output to another qualified buyer. Name the incentive, owner and measurable handoff at every step.
- **Limits:** framework and selected examples; drawing a circular diagram does not prove compounding. Low participation, long cycle times or acquisition costs can break the loop. Keep ordinary funnel reporting where useful.

## C08 — Monetization versus growth? It is a false choice

- **Source:** [Elena Verna, Dan Hockenmaier, Patrick Campbell and Brian Balfour, Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/monetization-vs-growth-its-a-false-choice).
- **Publication:** 2020-02-18. **Access:** public article; course not accessed.
- **Topics:** pricing, sales-assisted PLG, acquisition economics.
- **Mechanism:** packaging and price affect which acquisition and service models a business can afford; monetization is connected to distribution.
- **SignalDesk use:** check whether a proposed high-touch motion can be supported by actual contract economics. Where economics are unknown, show the missing inputs and proposed learning step instead of inventing CAC or payback.
- **Limits:** historical market claims and selected operator stories are not 2026 benchmarks. A successful price/package change elsewhere cannot establish causality or suitability for a new client.

## C09 — Net and gross revenue retention

- **Source:** [Aaron Wichman, ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/blog/net-and-gross-retention-rates/).
- **Publication:** 2022-12-14. **Access:** public article/product announcement.
- **Topics:** retention, expansion, finance.
- **Mechanism:** gross retention excludes expansion; net retention includes it. Both require a defined starting customer cohort and time period, excluding new customers acquired during that period.
- **SignalDesk use:** report customer loss separately from expansion so upsells cannot hide churn. Connect a renewal experiment to the correct cohort, renewal window and contract value.
- **Limits:** recurring-revenue metric definitions are not a diagnosis. Do not apply SaaS MRR formulas mechanically to project-based agencies. Billing, recognized revenue and cash remain distinct.

## C10 — Enhanced conversions for leads

- **Source:** [Google Ads official documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15713840?hl=en-AU).
- **Publication:** no article date displayed; includes a settings-change notice referring to April 2026. **Access:** public documentation.
- **Topics:** PPC, offline conversions, CRM, measurement.
- **Mechanism:** connect later offline lead outcomes with ad interactions using supported first-party identifiers and imports, rather than stopping measurement at the form submission.
- **SignalDesk use:** when authorized CRM and Ads access exist, define distinct lead and later commercial outcome events, stable identifiers and deduplication. Verify data quality before optimizing campaigns.
- **Limits:** attribution is not incrementality. Hashed data still requires appropriate collection/use permissions; documentation does not establish a client's compliance. Re-check current implementation requirements and account eligibility. No integration is implied by saving this reference.

## C11 — Information gain in SEO

- **Source:** [Louise Linehan, Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/information-gain/).
- **Publication:** 2026-08-20. **Access:** public article.
- **Topics:** organic, original evidence, differentiation.
- **Mechanism:** identify what useful information the audience cannot obtain from repetitive existing results, then contribute first-hand experience or original evidence.
- **SignalDesk use:** examine competing answers before proposing content. Prototype a genuinely useful comparison, demonstration or original observation; test buyer usefulness before producing a full asset or distributing it through paid/email channels.
- **Limits:** SEO vendor interpretation. The article explicitly says a literal Google information-gain ranking score is unconfirmed. A patent is not proof of implementation, and originality does not guarantee ranking, AI citations or revenue. Never invent original data to fill a gap.

## C12 — Benchmarks methodology and comparison controls

- **Source:** [ChartMogul Help Center](https://help.chartmogul.com/article/138-benchmarks).
- **Publication:** not established from inspected page. **Access:** public documentation; no account-level benchmark tool accessed.
- **Topics:** benchmarking, growth, retention, finance.
- **Mechanism:** compare recurring-revenue businesses through defined measures and segments, with year-over-year comparisons rather than an undifferentiated industry average.
- **SignalDesk use:** record metric definition, period, cohort and relevant company segment before using a benchmark. Show unavailable comparison inputs explicitly.
- **Limits:** participating vendor customer data is not the entire market. Subscription benchmarks can be inappropriate for services, early-stage experiments or different contract sizes. Association between retention and growth is not proof that a particular tactic caused either.

## C13 — How to think about paid ad experiments

- **Source:** [Abdallah Al-Hakim, Metadata](https://metadata.io/resources/blog/stop-struggling-start-experimenting-how-to-think-about-your-paid-ad-experiments).
- **Publication:** 2023-12-05. **Access:** public practitioner/vendor article.
- **Topics:** PPC, creative testing, pipeline, stopping rules.
- **Mechanism:** specify the demand objective and test a limited set of audience/message/offer differences, following outcomes beyond cost per lead.
- **SignalDesk use:** choose one decision, ensure the landing path works, set a spending cap, and compare qualified opportunity outcomes when data permits. Avoid proliferating test cells on small budgets.
- **Limits:** a particularly useful cautionary source: the article labels opportunity value divided by ad spend as “ROI.” SignalDesk must not repeat that label as profit or realized return. Vendor-selected observational data cannot prove that more experiments cause better results. Its large spending/timing recommendation is not a minimum for every small test; platform claims need fresh verification.

## C14 — Understand new user activation

- **Source:** [Bethany Mangan, Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/blog/understand-new-user-activation).
- **Publication:** 2023-04-11. **Access:** public tutorial.
- **Topics:** PLG, activation, retention, experiment design.
- **Mechanism:** define meaningful activation events, examine time and drop-off between them, compare behavioral cohorts, then test whether encouraging a behavior changes retention.
- **SignalDesk use:** distinguish initial setup from experienced value. Propose a small onboarding intervention tied to a specific observed obstacle, with later retention as a guardrail.
- **Limits:** vendor examples and correlation do not identify causal drivers by themselves. A reminder or sharing behavior may select already motivated users. The article's example counts/rates are not client benchmarks; without product analytics, activation and drop-off stay unknown.

## C15 — Expansion using customer data

- **Source:** [Priyanka Srinivasan, Gainsight](https://www.gainsight.com/blog/how-to-unlock-millions-of-dollars-in-expansion-using-customer-data/).
- **Publication:** 2019-08-20. **Access:** public vendor article.
- **Topics:** expansion, customer success, advocacy, sales enablement.
- **Mechanism:** connect actual entitlements, usage, customer outcomes and stakeholder knowledge before proposing an additional product or business-unit introduction.
- **SignalDesk use:** distinguish capacity limits, an unmet job and lack of awareness as different expansion hypotheses. Require customer value and an evidenced need before a commercial prompt.
- **Limits:** promotional headline and revenue claims are not a forecast. Missing purchases do not prove demand; similar accounts need not want the same bundle. This is ordinary expansion process guidance, not a novel experiment by itself. No automatic emails without authorized scope and valid recipient context.

## Combining references without recycling tactics

This is SignalDesk synthesis, not a claim the sources endorse a specific campaign:

1. Select a real buyer question from company evidence. If the buyer or problem is unknown, investigate that first.
2. Pair one distinctive interaction mechanism with one distribution mechanism and one appropriate measurement reference. For example, a new firsthand observation (C11), voluntary partner distribution (C03), and properly defined later conversion events (C10). That combination still needs company fit and a reason to participate.
3. Write what the buyer does differently. Reject anything that reduces to a renamed audit, nurture sequence, webinar or routine client review.
4. Validate the most consequential unknown cheaply; do not replace unavailable CRM data with someone else's benchmark.
5. Keep costs, potential opportunity value, signed bookings and recognized revenue separate. A useful learning test can succeed without a defensible revenue forecast.
